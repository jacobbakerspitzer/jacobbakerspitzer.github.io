Machine learning Â· Duke CatalystOct 2026 Â· Complete

Predicting Bank Sign-Ups

A model that predicts whether a bank customer will sign up for a term deposit after a marketing call, trained on about 31,000 real calls. The model is plain logistic regression. The real work was deciding, column by column, how to turn messy text and "unknown" answers into numbers the model could use.

ROC-AUC 0.79Pythonpandasscikit-learnSolo
How I prepared the data
  • Checked each column's sign-up rate before deciding how to encode it, and wrote down every decision with a reason
  • One-hot encoding for categories with no real order, like job and marital status
  • Grouped ages into ranges, because sign-ups dipped for middle-aged clients and rose sharply for older ones, a shape one number can't capture
  • Encoded month by call volume: the quietest months had sign-up rates near 50%, the busiest about 6%
  • Dropped call length. It's only known after the call ends, so using it would be cheating (data leakage)
Results on held-out test data
ScoreModel
Accuracy89.9%
Precision70.8%
Recall17.1%
F127.5%
ROC-AUC0.79

Only about 11% of customers say yes, so always guessing "no" already scores 88.7% accuracy. That's why I report all five scores: the model is usually right when it predicts a sign-up, but it misses most of them. Scores held up on a separate test set from the organizers.

Northwest Bronx in Data

I mapped housing and the economy in my home neighborhood (Bronx Community District 8: Riverdale, Kingsbridge and Marble Hill) and compared it with the Bronx and the US, using Census data and NYC's housing-violation records.

45,000+ records mappedPythonpandasgeopandasmatplotlib
How I did it
  • Combined four Census tables (2020â€“2024 estimates) for the 25 census tracts in my area
  • Matched each housing violation (no heat, leaks, pests) to its tract by latitude and longitude, since the records had no tract ID
  • Measured violations per renter household, so tracts with more apartments don't automatically look worse
  • Made maps of violations, rent burden, income and unemployment, then presented the findings in a short slide talk
