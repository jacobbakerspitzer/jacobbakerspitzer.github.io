About / Roster

Raised on New York courts and grids. Now building circuits and code in Durham.

I'm a first-year Electrical & Computer Engineering student at Duke, and economics is the other half of how I think. I grew up in New York City and went to NEST+m, which meant years of subways, gyms, and wondering how a city that big actually keeps running.

That question never really left. I like getting inside systems: circuits, software, buildings, markets, the grid under Manhattan. Before Duke I built a dataset of more than 1,000 New York buildings covering about a century of construction for an economics research paper, and I shadowed a structural engineer at The LiRo Group on real NYC projects. Both taught me that the interesting part is usually in how the pieces connect.

At Duke, my EGR 101 team is designing a fishing game for a young kid who needs to play with two switches instead of a normal controller. It's the first time I've designed something for one specific person, and it changes every decision. Along the way I'm getting better at Python, C++, CAD, circuits, and soldering.

Outside engineering, most of my life has been sports and music. I've refereed 180+ soccer matches, captained varsity soccer and tennis, earned a black belt in karate, and played baritone sax in the advanced jazz band. Every one of those has a rulebook. I like learning the rules well enough to play inside them, and eventually to build something new within them.
